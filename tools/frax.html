FRAX

10-year probability of a major osteoporotic or hip fracture, integrating clinical risk factors with optional femoral neck bone mineral density.

The clinical inputs FRAX uses

FRAX combines twelve inputs into its risk estimate. Having these ready before opening the official calculator makes the assessment quicker — the calculation itself still has to run on Sheffield's own tool, since the country-specific coefficients behind it were never published (see below).

1Age40–90 years
2SexSeparate models for men and women
3Weightkg
4Heightcm
5Previous fractureA fracture after age 40, occurring spontaneously or from trauma that wouldn't fracture a healthy bone
6Parent fractured hip
7Current smoking
8GlucocorticoidsCurrent or past exposure to oral glucocorticoids for > 3 months
9Rheumatoid arthritis
10Secondary osteoporosisA disorder strongly associated with osteoporosis (e.g. type 1 diabetes, untreated hyperthyroidism)
11Alcohol3 or more units per day
12Femoral neck BMDOptional — g/cm² or T-score, by scanner make if known

Why this links out instead of calculating directly

FRAX's underlying statistical model uses country-specific coefficients that the University of Sheffield has not published. Any independent implementation would necessarily be an approximation rather than the validated tool. Rather than offer an unverified substitute, this page links directly to the official calculator.
